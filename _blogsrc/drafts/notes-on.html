Recently I read an amazing article on Firstround about Career growth. I thought it might be useful to share my notes on the article so here I'm collecting my notes that I have shared on Twitter into this article.

Strive to be the most valuable, but least critical. Unless you would like to get a call at 2 AM for an incident, make sure you are adding amazing value without being a bottleneck.

Being a manager is emotionally intensive if you want to do it right. As an IC, you care more about building something and the more you progress, the easier things get comfortable till you start a new challenge. For people management, it isn't, especially managing a large team.

Manager's emotions go up and down with everything going across the team and there is always something that is broken. So better normalize your emotions and seek a support group that can help and mentors.

The skills required to succeed as a technical lead or manager are much more connected than you think. The ability to break down large problems and deliver on technical projects is a valuable skill that gives you the career flexibility to switch from one to the other.

Focus on learning and growth rather than moving up. Ask yourself, how can I get better? We learn when we make mistakes or see others doing something then apply the learnings to a new problem. Moving on from a company or a team too early can prevent you from learning some stuff.

Frameworks come and go, but fundamentals stand the test of time. Any investment in fundamentals is sure to give dividends throughout your career.

As a junior, you shall focus on shipping great work and finding good mentors and role models to learn from.

As a tech lead you need to soak all you can to ensure strong foundations regardless of the future path. You are the one who shall think about all the scenarios, prioritize the right things to work on and manage work with different people.

Don't just rely on being the domain expert. Your sew will lose its sharpness and you will be a complacent building sub-optimal solutions sooner than you think stuck with no growth trajectory.
